notes: "The amounts are enough for about 25 koláče (about 16 g each). For fewer, change the servings. Grinding: in Denmark poppy seed (birkes) is sold whole, in 50 g sachets, so for 200 g you need four sachets (two big or three small). The seeds must be ground, because whole ones won't bind with the milk and the filling stays grainy and flavourless. Use an electric coffee or spice grinder (kaffekværn) from Bilka, about 100–200 kr, and grind one 50 g sachet at a time. If you grind coffee in it too, wipe it first with a piece of dry bread or a spoon of rice. A stick blender can work if you first simmer the seeds in hot milk for about 30 minutes, but a regular blender can't grind them. The filling keeps for 3 days in the fridge and freezes well. Variations: a handful of raisins (rosiner) soaked in rum, or 1 tbsp honey in place of part of the sugar."

1. **Grind the poppy seeds.** Grind the poppy seeds in a coffee or spice grinder, one 50 g sachet at a time, in short pulses of about 3 seconds with a shake in between, about 5 times. A long run turns them into a paste. They are ready when they are grey-blue, smell strong and clump slightly when pinched.

2. **Cook with the milk.** Bring the milk to a boil in a small pot, add the ground poppy seeds and cook over low heat, stirring all the time, for about 5 minutes, until the seeds soak up the milk and it thickens into a porridge.

3. **Flavour.** Take it off the heat and stir in the sugar, butter, vanilla sugar, lemon zest and rum. If it is too thin, stir in the breadcrumbs until it is a thick paste that holds on a spoon.

4. **Cool.** Leave the filling to cool completely, about 30 minutes. A warm filling would soften the dough, and it thickens a little more as it cools.

5. **Use.** Put about 1 heaped teaspoon on each koláč. It is also good in [yeast buns](/recipes/kynute-buchty/) and with povidla or quark topped with crumble, as in the [koláče](/recipes/kynute-kolace/).
