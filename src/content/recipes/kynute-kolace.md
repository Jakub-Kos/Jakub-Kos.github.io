notes: "Adapted from iPrima Fresh's Klasické české kynuté koláče. The original uses a mix of 250 g hladká and 250 g polohrubá flour, which 500 g of ordinary hvedemel replaces in Denmark. iPrima gives no rising or baking times, so the ones here (75 minutes' rise, 15–18 minutes at 180 °C) are filled in from usual practice; watch the first tray. The recipe makes about 25 koláče and is split in three, about 8 of each filling. For the poppy filling, make the poppy seed filling recipe and set its servings to 8, which gives about 130 g. Quark: Danish kvark is runnier than Czech tvaroh, so drain it overnight in a sieve lined with a cloth, in the fridge. It is the same type of cheese, and you find it in the chilled aisle next to the yoghurt and skyr, usually from Arla. Don't buy hytteost (cottage cheese): it is grainy and doesn't work. If the filling is still thin after draining, stir in a teaspoon of vanilla custard powder (vaniljecremepulver) or cornstarch (majsstivelse). Povidla: you can't count on getting it in Denmark. Danish blommemarmelade is runnier and sweeter, so use less of it or boil it down until thick. A thicker substitute is to purée dried prunes (svesker) with a little water. Lard: you can leave it out and use 60 g of butter; if you want it, it's sold as svinefedt. Danish butter is often salted (Lurpak in the blue pack): look for \"usaltet\", or leave out the salt. Poppy seed: birkes is sold whole, so it must be ground at home (see the filling recipe). If you'd rather not bother, make all the koláče with povidla and quark. Honey is not needed here."

1. **Drain the quark.** The evening before, put the quark in a sieve lined with a cloth or tea towel and leave it in the fridge overnight. Danish kvark is much runnier than Czech tvaroh.

2. **Make the starter (kvásek).** Warm about 50 ml of the milk until lukewarm and crumble in the yeast. Add 1 tsp sugar, a pinch of salt and 1 tbsp of the flour. Stir, cover and leave it for 10 minutes to foam.

3. **Mix the dough.** In a bowl combine the flour, sugar, salt, lemon zest and the foamy starter. Add the soft butter (and lard), the egg yolk and the rest of the lukewarm milk gradually. If the dough is stiff, add a little more milk.

4. **Knead.** Knead for 15–20 minutes by hand (about 8 minutes in a mixer) until the dough is smooth, elastic and comes away from the bowl.

5. **Rise.** Dust the dough with flour, cover with a tea towel and leave somewhere warm until it has doubled, about 75 minutes. Tip from the original: heat the oven to about 35 °C, switch it off and put the bowl inside. It must not go above 40 °C.

6. **Make the fillings.** While the dough rises, make the [poppy seed filling](/recipes/makova-napln/) (set its servings to 8) and leave it to cool. Stir the drained quark with the egg yolk, vanilla sugar, lemon juice and zest and rum. Rub the butter, sugar and flour for the crumble together with your fingers until crumbly.

7. **Cut out.** Roll the risen dough into a sheet about 1 cm thick and cut out circles with a glass. Dip the base of the glass in flour and press a hollow into the middle of each koláč. Set them on trays lined with baking paper.

8. **Fill.** Brush the rims with the beaten egg white. Fill about a third of the koláče with a heaped teaspoon of poppy seed filling, another third with ½ tsp of povidla, and the last third with a level teaspoon of the quark filling. Sprinkle all of them with the crumble. Leave them for 15–20 minutes to rise again, and heat the oven to 200 °C (180 °C fan) meanwhile.

9. **Bake.** Put an ovenproof dish of water on the bottom of the oven for steam. Bake for 15–18 minutes, until the rims are golden.
