notes: "Cross-checked from vikendovepeceni.cz (Medové jidáše) and toprecepty.cz (Velikonoční jidáše). TopRecepty bakes for 20 minutes, which is too long for dough with honey in it, because honey browns fast; 180 °C for 13–16 minutes and watching them is enough. The butter and lemon in the glaze come from the reader comments and from Víkendové pečení, because plain honey just turns sticky. The shape is a double S: roll a rope of even thickness along its whole length (if the ends are thinner, the spirals unroll while rising). Pieces of 50 g give the size in the photo. Shape both ends at once, one hand on each, to get two spirals of the same size. Jidáše are best on the day they're baked. For a dry finish, dust them with powdered sugar instead of the honey glaze. Poppy seeds on top are from Víkendové pečení and are optional; classic jidáše are just honey. In Bilka, poppy seed is birkes (blå birkes, 50 g sachets). Whole seeds are right for sprinkling, so one sachet is plenty, and no grinding is needed. The honey to buy is a liquid one in a squeeze bottle (Jakobsens Flydende Honning or Salling ØKO Blomsterhonning, 250 g, which is enough for the dough and the glaze). There is no polohrubá mouka in Denmark, but ordinary hvedemel works fine. It may need a little less milk, so add the last of it gradually. Danish butter is often salted (Lurpak in the blue pack): look for \"usaltet\", or leave out the salt. Whole milk (sødmælk) gives a softer dough than letmælk."

1. **Make the starter (kvásek).** Crumble the yeast into about 50 ml of the lukewarm milk, add 1 tsp of the sugar and 1 tsp of the flour and stir. Leave it somewhere warm for 10 minutes, until it foams.

2. **Mix the dough.** Sift the flour into a bowl with the salt. Add the rest of the sugar, the honey, egg yolks, lemon zest and the foamy starter. Pour in the rest of the milk gradually while you knead; you may not need all of it, or you may need a little more. The dough should be soft, not stiff.

3. **Work in the butter.** Once the dough has come together, add the soft butter and knead for 8–10 minutes by hand (about 6 minutes in a mixer), until smooth, shiny and no longer sticking to the bowl.

4. **First rise.** Cover the bowl with a tea towel or film and leave the dough somewhere warm until it has doubled, usually 60–90 minutes.

5. **Divide.** Turn the dough out and cut it into 18 pieces of about 50 g; weighing them is the most exact way. Cover them so they don't dry out and let them rest for 5 minutes, which makes them easier to roll.

6. **Shape the S.** Roll each piece with your palms into a rope 25–28 cm long, the same thickness all along. Lay it straight on the worktop. Curl one end to the right into a snail and the other end to the left, until the two spirals meet in the middle. That gives an S with two spirals. Tuck the ends under the spirals so they don't unwind in the oven.

7. **Second rise.** Set the jidáše on a tray lined with baking paper, about 3 cm apart because they will still grow. Cover them and leave them for 20–30 minutes. Heat the oven to 190 °C (180 °C fan) meanwhile.

8. **Brush and bake.** Beat the egg with the milk and brush it gently over the jidáše. Sprinkle with poppy seeds if you like. Bake for 13–16 minutes until golden, and watch them, because the honey makes them brown fast.

9. **Glaze.** While they bake, warm the honey, butter and lemon juice gently in a small pan. Don't boil it. Brush it over the jidáše as soon as they come out of the oven, while they're hot, and leave them to cool on a rack.
